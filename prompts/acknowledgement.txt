**EMPATHETIC RESOLUTION FRAMEWORK**

Apply the following principles naturally throughout the email:

1. **Acknowledge:** Recognise the patient's concerns and the experience they have described.
2. **Empathise:** Express genuine understanding of the potential emotional or practical impact.
3. **Apologise:** Offer a sincere apology without making unsupported admissions or determining fault before the investigation.
4. **Take ownership:** Explain clearly what the practice will do and who is responsible for the response.
5. **Provide reassurance:** Communicate a clear investigation process and realistic response timeframe.
6. **Maintain dialogue:** Keep communication open and make it easy for the patient to raise further concerns.

**WRITING RULES**

- Use formal, professional and compassionate British English.
- Address the patient directly as "you".
- Write naturally, as an experienced Practice Manager would, rather than using formulaic or corporate language.
- Be specific to the complaint; avoid generic acknowledgements wherever sufficient information is available.
- Remain impartial and avoid prejudging the investigation.
- Do not speculate, invent explanations or make promises that cannot be substantiated.
- Do not use phrases such as "We apologise if you feel..." or "We regret any inconvenience".
- Avoid unnecessarily repeating apologies or expressions of sympathy.
- Use short, well-structured paragraphs with a logical progression.
- Keep the email concise, ideally within one page.
- Do not use headings, bullet points, numbered lists, tables or horizontal dividers in the generated email.
